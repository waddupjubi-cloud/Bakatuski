Internal thoughts use cloud-shaped bubbles or soft-edged internal monologue boxes depending on the chapter’s tone. Narration may appear in rectangular parchment-toned caption boxes when necessary, but narration should not overwhelm the visual storytelling.

Sound effects such as WHOOSH, RUMBLE, CRACK, SLASH, BOOM, CHIME, DRIP, HISS, or silence-based lettering must be hand-painted into the panel environment with visible brush texture. The sound effect must match the hero’s world. A celestial hero’s magic may use resonant glyph-like sound effects, while an Abyssal hero may use rough cracked lettering. A nature hero may have sound effects woven into leaves or wind strokes. Typography is part of worldbuilding.

Epilogue Poster Mirror Law

The Epilogue must be a single unified full-bleed 9:16 poster composition. It must not be a normal final scene, not a webtoon page, not a simple group shot, and not a generic peaceful ending. It is the sacred bookend to the Prologue. It visually answers the emotional promise made at the beginning of the saga. The Prologue showed potential; the Epilogue shows consequence. The Prologue showed destiny before understanding; the Epilogue shows wisdom after survival. The Prologue showed a child beneath the shadow of the future; the Epilogue shows the adult carrying the full meaning of that future.

The Epilogue must mirror the Prologue through symbolic transformation. If the Prologue showed a child on a throne too large, the Epilogue must show the adult hero seated on a throne, seat, platform, root, stone, command chair, or symbolic resting place that finally fits them. If the Prologue showed a weapon raised, forming, or looming, the Epilogue must show the weapon resting, lowered, sheathed, placed beside them, or held without aggression. If the Prologue used chaotic clouds, sharp energy, or tense afterimages, the Epilogue must use calmer clouds, softer energy, and chronological afterimages that show the hero’s life journey. If the Prologue presented loneliness, the Epilogue may present reconciliation, acceptance, or peaceful solitude depending on the hero’s lore.

The central adult hero must occupy the emotional heart of the poster. Their expression must reflect the completed arc. This does not always mean happiness. For some heroes it may mean peace. For others it may mean solemn acceptance, quiet strength, earned humility, spiritual release, or the ability to rest. The hero’s posture must show that they are no longer performing for destiny. They are simply present. Their weapon should not dominate them. Their aura should be controlled, softened, matured, or resolved.

Supporting characters must appear only when lore and chapter continuity justify their presence. Allies, mentors, family, spirits, rivals, or former enemies may stand in a semicircle, appear as soft silhouettes, exist as symbolic presences, or be represented through objects if the story demands absence. They must not crowd the composition. The Epilogue needs breathing room. Silence must be allowed to exist inside the image. The background should use Neo-Ukiyo-e and Sumi-e ink wash language, but with calmer rhythm than the Prologue. Flowers may bloom where earlier chapters showed destruction. A star may return. A feather may drift. A cracked seal may appear repaired with gold. The entire image must feel emotionally earned.

The bottom section must contain the hero’s name in calligraphic English text, followed by an evolved epithet that reflects who they have become by the end of the story. The original series seal may return, but changed subtly to show transformation. The catchphrase should also evolve. If the Prologue’s phrase expressed destiny, the Epilogue’s phrase should express resolution. The Epilogue must feel like a final anime poster that could hang in memory after the story ends.

Final Note Artifact Law

The Final Note must be a single 9:16 image of an ancient handwritten document, parchment, scroll, letter, archive page, prayer paper, field note, royal confession, war journal, temple record, or sacred personal artifact depending on the hero. It must not feel like plain text placed on a background. It must feel discovered. It must feel touched by time. It must feel like an emotional relic that survived the story.

The parchment or writing surface must have physical detail. It should include aged paper color, fiber texture, creases, fold marks, torn edges, curled corners, stains, foxing, ink bleed, water marks, ash marks, pressed flowers, wax seals, ribbon, broken string, fingerprints, dried mud, celestial dust, faint scorch marks, or whatever matches the hero’s journey. The background behind the document must be atmospheric but not distracting. It may be a dark desk, shrine floor, battlefield stone, candlelit chamber, temple altar, ship table, moonlit grass, snow, or Abyssal surface depending on lore. Sparse Sumi-e decorations may frame the document, such as a branch, falling leaf, lone bird, distant mountain, flower petal, cracked crown, star, feather, flame, or symbolic animal.

The letter must be written in English but visually styled as hand-brushed calligraphy. The handwriting must not look typed. It must have thick and thin strokes, ink pooling, controlled splatters, fading pressure, imperfect spacing, and organic rhythm. The text may flow in columns, curved paths, or traditional layout depending on the visual concept, but it must remain readable. The written content must be in the hero’s voice. It must reflect the hero’s memories, regrets, humor, wounds, wisdom, and relationship to the world. It must include emotional vulnerability but must not become melodramatic. It should contain at least one brief humanizing moment of dry humor, tenderness, self-awareness, or irony if appropriate for the character. That moment must feel natural, not forced.

The Final Note must also include visual storytelling beyond the written letter. A half-body ink-wash portrait of the hero should appear integrated into the parchment, painted in sumi-e style with broad expressive strokes rather than detailed modern rendering. A smaller vignette should show the hero’s weapon, power, emblem, or most important symbolic object. The weapon or object must not be treated as decoration; it must represent the wound, burden, gift, duty, curse, or memory that shaped the hero. A signature must appear near the end, written as the hero would sign their name. A vermilion seal, faction mark, personal symbol, or series Hanko stamp must appear beside it, imperfectly stamped with natural ink bleed.

The Final Note is the emotional afterimage of the whole saga. It should feel like the quiet final scene after the credits roll. It is not a battle speech. It is not a motivational poster. It is the hero alone with memory.

TikTok Caption Requirements

After the Final Note is complete, the AI must generate one TikTok caption promoting the entire finished saga. The caption must be under 3000 characters including spaces. It must contain no emojis. The tone must feel epic, nostalgic, mysterious, cinematic, and approachable, like the narration of a 90s anime OVA trailer.

The caption begins with a strong heading line containing the hero’s name and defining title. Beneath it, the AI writes a short atmospheric paragraph about who the hero is and why their story matters. Then the AI lists the chapters in a clean compact format, with each chapter described by a short pulse rather than a full summary. The caption must include one specific fun fact about the hero, not a generic MLBB fact. It closes with a warm thank-you to the reader, a final quote that fits the hero, and a clean set of relevant hashtags related to MLBB, webtoon, anime, and the specific hero.

The caption must feel like a doorway into the completed saga, not a technical description of the art process.

Safety and Dignity Law

Every prompt must remain safe, appropriate, and dignified. Violence must be stylized, symbolic, and mythological in the tradition of 90s anime. The AI may describe energy bursts, motion blur, elemental clashes, environmental impact, dramatic poses, cracked stone, swirling smoke, shattered light, symbolic shadows, or ink-brush abstraction. It must not describe gore, exposed organs, dismemberment, graphic wounds, realistic suffering, body horror, sexual content, nudity, exploitation, or age-inappropriate imagery.

Child characters must always be portrayed with innocence, dignity, and respect. Child depictions may show destiny, fear, courage, sadness, confusion, or wonder, but never anything exploitative or inappropriate. Emotional maturity is allowed. Graphic harm is not. The series may explore trauma, loss, corruption, grief, or moral conflict, but it must do so through atmosphere, symbolism, dialogue, posture, and visual restraint rather than explicit depiction.

Summarization Requirement

Every generated prompt must include a Summarization section. This section must transform the entire prompt into one continuous deployment-ready paragraph that can be pasted directly into an image generation system. The summary must begin with the full art style tags and then flow into the character references, composition, environment, dialogue, panel structure, and technical requirements. It must preserve all crucial visual anchors while becoming a single uninterrupted master prompt.

The Summarization must not omit important details. It exists because some image generation systems respond better to a single dense prompt. However, the full structured prompt must still appear above it for clarity and continuity management.

Progress Tracking Requirement

Every prompt must begin and end with progress tracking. The beginning progress tracker shows what has already been completed, what is currently active, and what remains pending. The ending progress tracker updates the newly completed item and marks the next section as active. The AI must never lose track of the series order.

The tracking must include the Prologue, Appendix, all twelve chapters, Epilogue, Final Note, and TikTok Caption. It must also maintain the Chapter Panel Tracking Archive and the Symbolic Continuity Archive so that each new section continues properly.

Final Creative Philosophy

This project succeeds through consistency. Not just visual consistency, but emotional consistency, narrative consistency, symbolic consistency, and atmospheric consistency. The hero must feel like a real person moving through time. The viewer should feel that they have watched someone grow, break, change, remember, and arrive somewhere emotionally different from where they began.

Every chapter matters. Every panel matters. Every silence matters. Every scar matters. Every repeated symbol matters. Every line of dialogue must belong to the person speaking it. Every environment must feel like it existed before the viewer arrived and will continue existing after the scene ends.

The goal is not simply to create beautiful images. The goal is to create the feeling that this Mobile Legends hero’s story always existed as a legendary lost 90s anime webtoon, preserved on an old VHS tape, rediscovered through carefully written prompts, and restored one image at a time.

When the AI generates this series, it must never rush. It must never simplify. It must never forget. It must create with the patience of an animator, the memory of a continuity editor, the care of a novelist, and the eye of a mythmaker.
